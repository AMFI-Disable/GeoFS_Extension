## Verdict

**Leg 3 fails on GeoFS V4.** The permission prompt, automatic boost and thrust restoration worked. SAS did not maintain the captured attitude to the required stability, and inverted gear telemetry makes the pre-cruise gear check incorrect. Cruise altitude and Mach checks responded to live thresholds.

This completes the attempted live verification for this leg, with the limits below. It does not establish a successful smooth climb through cruise, calibrated speed-compensated turn behavior, or GeoFS V3 compatibility. No controller fixes were made in this task.

## Confirmed Findings

### High: Gear State Is Inverted

The native functions establish the convention directly: `setGearUp.set()` changes target from 0 to 1; `setGearDown.set()` changes target from 1 to 0. Native animation `gearPosition` follows that position. However, `SimAdapter.buildTelemetrySnapshot` sets `gearDown` when `gearPosition > 0.5`.

| Native State | Extension Telemetry | PRE-CRZ Gear Row |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gear UP, position 1 / target 1 | `gearDown: true` | `Landing Gear - UP & OFF` unchecked |
| Gear commanded DOWN, position about 0.053 / target 0 | `gearDown: false` | `Landing Gear - UP & OFF` automatically checked |

The gear row was not clicked to force manual completion. The incorrect result came from native gear commands and live telemetry. This inversion also affects any other feature using normalized `gearDown`; those downstream effects were not exhaustively tested.

### High: SAS Does Not Maintain Its Captured Attitude Under The Requested Boost

`SAS_ACTIVE` was emitted and live settings confirmed SAS enabled. The 500x setting was confirmed before activating manual thrust. Both rated engine thrusts increased from 7,700 to 3,850,000 and restored to 7,700 after deactivation.

During a roughly two-second 500x pulse at a commanded low throttle of 0.02:

- SAS target stayed near pitch 2.736 degrees and roll 0.399 degrees.
- Indicated pitch moved from about 1.45 to a peak of 13.49 degrees, and roll moved from about 1.02 to -1.43 degrees.
- KIAS increased from about 239 to 468; altitude decreased from about 8,660 to 8,513 ft, with final vertical speed around -12,404 ft/min.
- Recorded roll, raw pitch and yaw input were zero throughout the pulse. No crash was reported.

This failed the requested smooth climb and steady attitude criterion; the high-thrust pulse was stopped rather than represented as a successful climb. Earlier hands-off sampling also showed pitch about -0.56 degrees against the 2.736-degree target, improving only to about 0.32 degrees over 12 seconds.

A later turn-and-release test with native AP explicitly off provides a separate observation: after release, SAS captured pitch -4.269 and bank -54.120 degrees. With all sampled pilot axes zero and native AP false, pitch moved to about -3.641 degrees and bank -53.809 degrees by the final sample roughly six seconds after release. Target capture and angular-rate damping occur, but the strict hold criterion did not pass. These results do not isolate a particular control-law defect or prove identical behavior in a clean profile.

### Low: Stopping A/THR Omits The Release Diagnostic

Observed `OPSYNC_PROMPT`, `OPSYNC_DECISION` with `allow-once`, and `OPSYNC_ENGAGED`. Stopping A/THR restored both rated thrusts to 7,700 and cleared automatic boost, but no `OPSYNC_RELEASED` diagnostic appeared in the captured stream. The `stop()` implementation emits the internal inactive event without emitting that diagnostic. Physical restoration passed; diagnostic completeness did not.

## Measurement Limits

- Native `flyTo` repositioning was used for altitude/speed threshold checks. These are live simulator state checks, not a continuously flown climb from the Leg 2 runway roll.
- Initial higher-speed attempts acquired nonzero control input after release. Native AP was subsequently found on. Those attempts were excluded from claims about hands-off SAS stability. Later samples explicitly recorded native AP false. The valid higher-speed sample still had a different initial bank, so it does not establish a controlled speed-compensation comparison.
- Automatic boost permission was observed for Allow Once. The other three policy decisions were displayed but not exercised individually.
- Sampling used current extension telemetry and native body/control state. Browser scheduling caused uneven sample intervals. Zero-input and native AP observations apply only to the periods explicitly captured.
- The temporary post-load window error listener recorded no uncaught errors. Earlier startup failures, console-only errors, and all third-party script interactions were not excluded.
